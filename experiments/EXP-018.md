# EXP-018 — What of an earlier filesystem survives a reformat or an fsid change

- **Date:** 2026-10-07 (registration, committed before the measured builds; results the same day)
- **Milestone:** M6f (plan.md), issue #54
- **Script:** [`experiments/exp018.py`](exp018.py); environment: [`experiments/env.sh`](env.sh)
- **Raw outputs:** `images/scratch/exp/EXP-018/results.jsonl` and `control.jsonl` (gitignored)
- **Registration:** §1 to §3 and §5 were committed before the measured builds. **What was known
  beforehand**, so the reader can judge how blind this is: five pilot images built while writing
  the scenarios (`images/scratch`, deleted), and one build of each corpus row (`./setup.sh`), all
  read with the foreign scan. On those, the scan found the old fsid on both reformats (196 and 218
  valid blocks), 30 valid blocks of the pre-change fsid after `btrfstune -u`, none after
  `btrfstune -m`; a reformat with the same options followed by a second life left 2 old blocks.
  The predictions below are those observations; this experiment measures whether they hold across
  regenerations and how much the counts vary.

## 1. Hypothesis

A reformat with the pinned mkfs.btrfs and an fsid change with `btrfstune -u` leave tree blocks
of the earlier filesystem (the earlier fsid) on the device; the foreign-FSID scan finds them,
validates them in their own geometry and tells the two apart; `btrfstune -m` leaves none.

Per row of corpus/manifest.tsv, in each of N = 5 regenerations:
- **H1 (`m6_reformat`, same options, no second life).** The old fsid (the first life's, from the
  serial log) is found, its context is `inferred` with the old geometry (16384, xxhash), its kind
  is `reformat`, and its valid blocks number more than 100.
- **H2 (`m6_reformat_geometry`, crc32c 32 KiB to mixed xxhash 4 KiB, 60 MiB, second life).** The
  old fsid is found with context `superblock` (mirror 1) and geometry (32768, crc32c), kind
  `reformat`, no invalid block, more than 100 valid.
- **H3 (`m6_fsid_u`).** The pre-change fsid is found, kind `fsid_change`, at least 10 valid
  blocks; its invalid blocks are mkfs's never-written ones (at most 5).
- **H4 (`m6_fsid_m`).** No foreign filesystem; the metadata_uuid change is reported.
- **H5 (no false finding).** No foreign filesystem other than the old fsid on any build, and on
  the control (sandbox.img and every other corpus image) none, except the transplanted mirror of
  `m1_foreign_mirror` (EXP-017), reported with no block. No image changes.

**What would refute it.** Any build where the old fsid is missed or misidentified, a geometry
other than the predicted one, a foreign filesystem on a clean image, or a changed image. Counts
outside the predicted bounds refute the counts, not the identification.

**What it means for the paper.** A reformatted or re-identified device still holds validatable
metadata of the earlier filesystem, which a reader that keys on the current fsid (every reader we
know of, btrfska's `scan` included) never sees; and the two histories can be told apart from what
survives.

## 2. Method

For each build index 1 to 5, each row is rebuilt with its own driver and the manifest's options
(`corpus/vm/scenarios/reformat.sh`, `fsid_change.sh`) under `images/scratch/exp/EXP-018/builds/`.
The serial log's `=== FS` line of the first life gives the old fsid and geometry. The image is
hashed, `foreign_scan` runs over the targeted scan plan, the image is hashed again. Recorded per
image: every foreign filesystem's fsid, kind, context source and geometry, candidates, valid,
invalid, generations and device uuids; the metadata_uuid change. `control` does the same without
a log on sandbox.img and the other corpus images. The table gives, per row, the kinds seen, the
contexts seen, and the median and range of the old fsid's valid and invalid blocks.

## 3. Image / scenario

Rows `m6_reformat`, `m6_reformat_geometry`, `m6_fsid_u`, `m6_fsid_m` of corpus/manifest.tsv
(plan.md M6f, corpus): guest kernel 7.0.0-31-generic, pinned mkfs.btrfs and btrfstune of
btrfs-progs v6.6.3, guest scenario `foreign`, mount options `commit=5`. Every build draws new
UUIDs, so no image hash identifies a build beyond itself.
