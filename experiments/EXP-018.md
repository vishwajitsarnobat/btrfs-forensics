# EXP-018 — What of an earlier filesystem survives a reformat or an fsid change

- **Date:** 2026-10-07 (registration, committed as `40f7489` and pushed before the measured
  builds; results the same day)
- **Milestone:** M6f (plan.md), issue #54
- **Script:** [`experiments/exp018.py`](exp018.py); environment: [`experiments/env.sh`](env.sh)
- **Raw outputs:** `images/scratch/exp/EXP-018/results.jsonl` and `control.jsonl` (gitignored)
- **Registration:** §1 to §3 and §5 were committed before the measured builds. **What was known
  beforehand**, so the reader can judge how blind this is: five pilot images built while writing
  the scenarios (`images/scratch`, deleted), and one build of each corpus row (`./setup.sh`), all
  read with the foreign scan. On those, the scan found the old fsid on both reformats (196 and 218
  valid blocks), 30 valid blocks of the pre-change fsid after `btrfstune -u`, none after
  `btrfstune -m`; a reformat with the same options followed by a second life left 2 old blocks.
  The predictions below are those observations; this experiment measures whether they hold across
  regenerations and how much the counts vary.

## 1. Hypothesis

A reformat with the pinned mkfs.btrfs and an fsid change with `btrfstune -u` leave tree blocks
of the earlier filesystem (the earlier fsid) on the device; the foreign-FSID scan finds them,
validates them in their own geometry and tells the two apart; `btrfstune -m` leaves none.

Per row of corpus/manifest.tsv, in each of N = 5 regenerations:
- **H1 (`m6_reformat`, same options, no second life).** The old fsid (the first life's, from the
  serial log) is found, its context is `inferred` with the old geometry (16384, xxhash), its kind
  is `reformat`, and its valid blocks number more than 100.
- **H2 (`m6_reformat_geometry`, crc32c 32 KiB to mixed xxhash 4 KiB, 60 MiB, second life).** The
  old fsid is found with context `superblock` (mirror 1) and geometry (32768, crc32c), kind
  `reformat`, no invalid block, more than 100 valid.
- **H3 (`m6_fsid_u`).** The pre-change fsid is found, kind `fsid_change`, at least 10 valid
  blocks; its invalid blocks are mkfs's never-written ones (at most 5).
- **H4 (`m6_fsid_m`).** No foreign filesystem; the metadata_uuid change is reported.
- **H5 (no false finding).** No foreign filesystem other than the old fsid on any build, and on
  the control (sandbox.img and every other corpus image) none, except the transplanted mirror of
  `m1_foreign_mirror` (EXP-017), reported with no block. No image changes.

**What would refute it.** Any build where the old fsid is missed or misidentified, a geometry
other than the predicted one, a foreign filesystem on a clean image, or a changed image. Counts
outside the predicted bounds refute the counts, not the identification.

**What it means for the paper.** A reformatted or re-identified device still holds validatable
metadata of the earlier filesystem, which a reader that keys on the current fsid (every reader we
know of, btrfska's `scan` included) never sees; and the two histories can be told apart from what
survives.

## 2. Method

For each build index 1 to 5, each row is rebuilt with its own driver and the manifest's options
(`corpus/vm/scenarios/reformat.sh`, `fsid_change.sh`) under `images/scratch/exp/EXP-018/builds/`.
The serial log's `=== FS` line of the first life gives the old fsid and geometry. The image is
hashed, `foreign_scan` runs over the targeted scan plan, the image is hashed again. Recorded per
image: every foreign filesystem's fsid, kind, context source and geometry, candidates, valid,
invalid, generations and device uuids; the metadata_uuid change. `control` does the same without
a log on sandbox.img and the other corpus images. The table gives, per row, the kinds seen, the
contexts seen, and the median and range of the old fsid's valid and invalid blocks.

## 3. Image / scenario

Rows `m6_reformat`, `m6_reformat_geometry`, `m6_fsid_u`, `m6_fsid_m` of corpus/manifest.tsv

## 6. Results

N = 5 regenerations of each row; median (range) of the old fsid's blocks.

| Row | Kind (all builds) | Context (nodesize, csum type) | Old-fsid valid | Old-fsid invalid |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `m6_reformat` | `reformat` | inferred (16384, xxhash64) | 196 (196-196) | 0 (0-0) |
| `m6_reformat_geometry` | `reformat` | superblock mirror 1 (32768, crc32c) | 218 (218-218) | 0 (0-0) |
| `m6_fsid_u` | `fsid_change` | inferred (16384, xxhash64) | 30 (30-32) | 5 (5-5) |
| `m6_fsid_m` | no foreign filesystem; metadata_uuid change reported in 5 of 5 | - | - | - |

- **H1 holds.** `m6_reformat`: the old fsid in every build, inferred in its own geometry (32 of
  32 sampled blocks verify), `reformat` from the generations alone: no old chunk-tree leaf
  survives (the new mkfs writes its chunk tree where the old one was), and the old blocks reach
  generation 14 while the new filesystem is at 6.
- **H2 holds.** `m6_reformat_geometry`: the old mirror-1 superblock survives in every build (the
  new filesystem ends at 60 MiB), so the context is read from it; 28 old chunk-tree items name
  the old device uuid and none the current one. All 218 old blocks lie beyond the new filesystem,
  in the unmapped gap. This is EXP-017's foreign superblock on a real reformat, with its trees.
- **H3 holds.** `m6_fsid_u`: `fsid_change` in every build: in each, 31 old chunk-tree items
  name the current device uuid. The 5 invalid blocks are mkfs's generation-1 blocks without the
  WRITTEN flag, in every build.
- **H4 holds.** `m6_fsid_m`: no foreign header in any build; the change is read from the
  superblock (fsid differs from metadata_uuid).
- **H5 holds.** No other foreign filesystem on any build. Control, 25 images (sandbox.img and
  every other corpus image; `m1_unknown_incompat` is refused by the feature gate and counted as
  such): a foreign filesystem only on `m1_foreign_mirror`, its transplanted superblock, with no
  block.

## 7. Threats to validity

- **Internal.** The reformat counts did not vary at all across builds and the fsid-change count
  by 2: the scenario is short and mostly deterministic, so the spread says little about other
  workloads. The scan reads each image once; it is a deterministic parse of a fixed image.
- **External.** These images are files: on a regular file mkfs trims nothing (BLKDISCARD fails)
  and zeroes only the first 2 MiB and the superblock copies inside its size (btrfs-progs v6.6.3
  common/device-utils.c:121, 270-293). **On a block device that supports discard, mkfs trims the
  whole device by default** (l.270-278) unless `-K` is given, and a trimmed SSD usually returns
  zeros: a reformat there may leave nothing for this scan. Not measured. The second life of
  `m6_reformat_geometry` writes only below 60 MiB; a second life over the whole device, as in a
  pilot with the same options as `m6_reformat`, left 2 old blocks (§ registration). One guest
  kernel, one btrfs-progs version, one host.
- **Construct.** "Found" means valid in the inferred or superblock context without expectations
  (checksum, fsid, layout, WRITTEN, nritems); it is not a walk, and no file of the old filesystem
  is read back here. `fsid_change` rests on the device uuid; `mkfs.btrfs --device-uuid` can give a
  new filesystem the old device's uuid, and the scan would then call a reformat an fsid change
  unless the generations contradict it (they are then reported as `undetermined`).

## 8. Status

**Supports** the hypothesis on these images. What the paper may say: on reformats and fsid
changes built in the guest with the pinned tools (N = 5 each), the earlier filesystem's tree
blocks survive (196 and 218 valid blocks after a reformat without and with a second life inside
a smaller filesystem; 30 to 32 after `btrfstune -u`), the foreign-FSID scan validates every one in
its own geometry and identifies a reformat or an fsid change correctly in every build, and reports
nothing foreign on 23 clean images. Follow-ups: a reformat on a discard-capable block device;
reading the old filesystem's trees and files (its own chunk map and roots) from these blocks.
