# EXP-021 — The hiding detector on planted images: detection, stability and false positives

- **Date:** 2026-10-07 (registration; results below it once measured)
- **Milestone:** M6e (plan.md), issue #53; claim C5
- **Script:** [`experiments/exp021.py`](exp021.py); environment: [`experiments/env.sh`](env.sh)
- **Raw outputs:** `images/scratch/exp/EXP-021/*.jsonl` (gitignored)
- **Registration:** §1 and §2 were committed before the script was written or run. **What was
  known beforehand**, so the reader can judge how blind this is: M6d's tests (one build per corpus
  row) found each of the twelve planted rows with its technique at the planted bytes and nothing
  else; the 27 clean kept images and `sandbox.img` gave no finding except two `device_slack` on
  `m6_reformat_geometry` (the replaced, larger filesystem); the hide-and-seek images gave what
  plan.md M6d lists. While writing this record the detector was run once on the seven bases
  named below: no finding on any. Nothing had been planted at other sizes or positions, nothing
  planted had been mounted, and no fresh build had been read by the detector.

## 1. Hypothesis

Each rule of `btrfska hiding` reports exactly what mkfs.btrfs and the kernel never write, so it
finds every payload a hider puts where the rule looks, at the planted bytes, in every csum type
and base image, and reports nothing on a filesystem only mkfs.btrfs and the kernel wrote.

- **H1 (detection).** Over every plant of §2 (14 techniques, 7 bases, 4 csum types, payloads of
  one byte to the area's capacity, at its first byte, its last byte and a random place), every
  plant whose bytes lie in what the rule examines is reported with the planted technique (true
  positive rate 1.0 per technique), and a finding covers the planted bytes. The location is
  exact (first non-zero byte reported = first planted byte; non-zero bytes reported = bytes
  planted) for every area rule, **except** `sys_chunk_array_slack`, whose finding starts at the
  end of the live array and also counts the stale tail mkfs left (45 to 51 bytes on every image),
  and `device_slack`, which lists 4 KiB runs (the count is still exact). Registered misses, by
  design of the rules:
  - `timestamp_nsec`: a payload that leaves every nanosecond field below 10^9 and does not make
    the four fields four different printable values is not reported (a hider who knows the rule
    has 29.9 bits per field). Plants of that kind are counted apart, not as misses of the rule;
  - `node_slack`: a payload behind a plausible stale item header (a copy of the leaf's own last
    item header) is counted as stale slack and not reported;
  - `file_slack`: no plant is possible on bases whose files are all compressed or inline (the
    `m1_*` and `s01` images mount with `compress=zstd`); the rule does not cover compressed
    extents.
  Any other plant not reported refutes H1 for that technique, and is a detector bug to fix.
- **H2 (no collateral).** A plant produces findings of its own technique only, except where the
  same bytes are two techniques by definition: a one-copy plant in an inode item gives
  `copy_divergence` and, when the planted copy is the one read, `inode_reserved`; an overwritten
  mirror-1 slot gives `superblock_slot` only.
- **H3 (stability; N = 5 guest runs per technique and workload).** Planted images pass the pinned
  `btrfs check --readonly` and `--check-data-csum`, mount, and give no btrfs error in the kernel
  log, except the `file_slack` plant that keeps the old data checksum (check-data-csum, the
  read-only scrub and the read of the file report a csum error). After `use` (read every file,
  create, modify and delete files, read-only scrub):
  - survive and are still reported, in all five runs: `pre_superblock`, `superblock_reserved`
    (reserved bytes and gated fields), `superblock_padding`, `sys_chunk_array_slack`,
    `device_slack`, `string_item` (a real item, copied whole on copy-on-write), `file_slack` (the
    file is only read), `hidden_name`;
  - gone in all five runs: `node_slack` (the kernel zeroes slack on every write: EXP-005),
    `backup_root_divergence` and backup-slot padding (each commit zeroes and refills a slot, and
    the workload commits more than four times), `superblock_slot` (every commit writes every
    mirror), `copy_divergence` (the leaf is rewritten from one copy), `inode_reserved` and
    `timestamp_nsec` (reading the file updates its atime under `relatime`, and the kernel writes
    the whole inode item from a zeroed copy: delayed-inode.c). This contradicts Toolan &
    Humphries' "high" stability for inode reserved bytes; they may not have read the file.

  After `balance` (`use`, then a full balance), the same, and in addition: `file_slack` survives
  at the relocated extent (relocation copies whole sectors and their checksums),
  `sys_chunk_array_slack` survives (the array is rewritten in place, the payload lies past its
  end). `device_slack`: uncertain, no prediction (a new chunk may be placed over it).
  Nothing planted is reported by `btrfs check` after use (the kernel's own writes are clean).
- **H4 (false positives).** On every clean kept image, `sandbox.img`, the clean hide-and-seek
  member, and every fresh build (5 per recipe of §2), the detector reports nothing, except
  `device_slack` twice on `m6_reformat_geometry` (explained: bytes of the replaced filesystem)
  and the U+FEFF snapshot on `m6_hidden_snapshot` builds (planted). The false-positive rate is 0
  for every technique; reported with the number of images and the units examined.
- **H5 (hide-and-seek).** As M6d found: `superblock_slot` on `btrfs_superblock`, five
  `inode_reserved` at the metadata's offsets and one `copy_divergence` on
  `btrfs_inode_reserved`, the U+FEFF directory on `btrfs_hidden_snapshot`, `device_slack` on
  dev2 of `btrfs_raid1_slack`, nothing on dev1.

**What would refute it.** H1: a plant in a rule's scope not reported, or reported elsewhere. H2:
a finding of an unplanted technique. H3: a technique surviving where predicted gone, or gone
where predicted to survive, in any run; a planted image failing `btrfs check` or mount (then the
plant tests corruption, not hiding). H4: any finding on a clean image not explained.

**What it means for the paper.** C5 claims a detector for the published techniques. H1 and H4 are
its detection and false-positive rates; H3 says which techniques matter on a filesystem that was
used after the hiding (a detector for a technique the kernel erases still matters for an image
taken right after it), and the registered misses are what the paper must state as evasions.

## 2. Method

**Bases** (all clean, kept corpus images): `m1_xxhash` (xxhash), `m1_sha256_bgt` (sha256,
block-group tree), `m1_blake2b` (blake2b), `m6_datacsum` (crc32c, data DUP), `s01_discard_none_r1`
(xxhash, the oldest pipeline), `m3_wide` (internal nodes, 48 subvolumes), `m4_deep` (deep history,
log trees dropped).

**Plants.** Each technique's planter is the `corpus/mutate.py` function of M6d, with the position
and size as keyword arguments (plan.md M6e). Payload bytes are drawn from 1-255 by
`random.Random(21)` and a per-plant label, so every planted byte is non-zero and the planted range
is known from the patches (the bytes that differ from the base, checksum fields left out). Per
technique and base:
- `superblock_reserved`: reserved[199] (1 byte at its first and its last byte, a random size and
  place, all 199 bytes); each gated field whose flag is clear (1 byte first, 1 byte last, whole
  field); the padding of a backup-root slot (`unused_64`, `unused_8`, 1 byte and whole);
- `superblock_padding`: 565 bytes, as reserved[199];
- `sys_chunk_array_slack`: from the first byte after the stale tail to the end of the 2048-byte
  array: 1 byte first, 1 byte last, random, all of it;
- `superblock_slot`: the mirror-1 slot overwritten: one byte over the magic, 72 bytes from its
  start, all 4096 bytes;
- `pre_superblock`: 1 byte at 512 and at 65535, 1 byte at 69632 (after the primary superblock)
  and at 1048575, random in each area, both areas whole;
- `backup_root_divergence`: each older slot copied over the slot of the superblock's generation;
- `node_slack`: the fs tree's root node (when it has one) and first leaf, every copy: 1 byte at the
  slack's first byte and at its last, random, the whole slack; and the stale-header variant;
- `copy_divergence`: an inode-reserved plant applied to one copy of the leaf, each copy in turn;
- `inode_reserved`: 1 byte first, 1 byte last, random, 32 bytes;
- `timestamp_nsec`: 16 random bytes; 1 byte into the top byte of a field (value >= 10^9 and
  below); the low 3 bytes of all four fields; four different printable values below 10^9;
- `string_item`: 1 byte, random, the leaf's whole free space;
- `file_slack`: on the first top-directory file with a tail in an uncompressed regular extent:
  1 byte right after the end of file, 1 byte at the sector's last byte, random, the whole tail;
  each with the data checksum recomputed, and the whole tail once with it kept;
- `device_slack`: 1 byte at the first and the last byte past the last device extent (no
  superblock slot, no historical chunk), random, 1 MiB;
- `hidden_name`: five fresh builds of the guest scenario `hidden`, the kept `m6_hidden_snapshot`.

A plant the base cannot take (a gated field in use, no file tail, no internal node) is recorded
as not applicable with the planter's reason. Each planted image is a copy of the base in
`images/scratch/exp/EXP-021/` with the patches written into it, read once with
`hiding.detect.detect` (historical chunk maps as `btrfska hiding` discovers them) and deleted.

**Metrics.** True positive: a finding of the planted technique. Covers: a finding of it whose
area contains the first planted byte. Exact: as H1. Collateral: findings of other techniques.
Rates per technique: plants detected / plants in the rule's scope; the out-of-scope plants of H1
are listed apart.

**Stability.** One plant per technique (§2's default size and place, the M6d message) on the
M6d row's base, plus `superblock_slot`, `copy_divergence`, `file_slack` with the checksum kept,
a gated field and a backup-slot padding; `hidden_name` is the kept `m6_hidden_snapshot`. The
pinned `btrfs check --readonly` and `--readonly --check-data-csum` (btrfs-progs 6.6.3,
`corpus/vm/pinned.sh`) run once on the planted image (a pure read). Then, five times per workload,
a fresh copy is mounted in the guest (`run_scenario.sh`, mount options `commit=5`) with scenario
`stability_use` or `stability_balance`; the serial log gives mount success, the scrub summary and
every BTRFS kernel message; afterwards `btrfs check` (both modes), the detector, and a search of
the image for the payload. Survives: the technique is still reported. Payload on image: the
payload's bytes are found anywhere on the image.

**False positives.** The detector on every kept clean image (the corpus rows minus the planted
ones), `sandbox.img`, `btrfs_raid1_slack_dev1`, and five fresh builds of each recipe: the `s01`
trio, `m1_sha256_bgt`, `m1_blake2b`, `m2_logtree`, `m3_wide`, `m4_deep`, `m5_delsubvol`,
`m5_reuse`, `m6_datacsum`, `m6_hidden_snapshot`. Units examined per technique, from the
detector's summary: superblock copies (and their reserved, padding and array-tail bytes), slots,
boot-area bytes, block copies, inodes, file tails, device bytes past the last extent, directory
entries (counted by the script from the DIR_INDEX items of the current trees).
