## 1. Hypothesis

Across ≥ 5 regenerations of scenario s01, the median stale-block count with `discard=sync` is below
20 % of the median without discard, the async row (discard enabled, unmount about a second after
the last write) has the same medians as the no-discard row, and the values in research.md §10.4
equal the medians.

## 2. Method

- **Procedure.** One run is `RUN=<n> corpus/vm/discard_table.sh`. It builds three fresh images in
  sequence, one per row, and prints one probe line per row. `exp000.py run --runs 15` executes runs
  1–15 strictly one after the other (no parallel guests). Each image is a new 512 MiB file:
  truncate, host `mkfs.btrfs --csum xxhash`, then the stock 7.0 guest runs scenario s01
  (research.md §10.4) and unmounts.
- **Rows.**
  - `none`: virtio disk without `discard=unmap`; mount options `compress=zstd,commit=5`.
  - `async`: `DISCARD=1` (virtio `discard=unmap`), same mount options. The kernel enables
    `discard=async` itself on a discard-capable device.
  - `sync`: `DISCARD=1` and `compress=zstd,commit=5,discard=sync`.
- **Metrics** (the four probe columns, definitions in the probe's docstring):
  1. `fsid_blocks`: 4 KiB-aligned blocks with the superblock fsid at +0x20, superblock copies
     skipped;
  2. `stale_blocks`: the subset of (1) whose header generation is below the primary superblock's;
  3. `needle_copies`: byte-exact occurrences of `small secret`, the deleted inline file's content;
  4. `nonzero_blocks`: 4 KiB blocks with any non-zero byte.
- **N and statistics.** N = 15 per row (the plan asks for ≥ 5; the first 5 runs showed no spread,
  so 10 more were run to estimate how often the previously seen jitter occurs). Per row and
  column: median and range (min–max).
- **Success.** All three clauses of the hypothesis hold.
- **Representative image per row** (kept for EXP-002 and the manifest; all other images and logs
  were deleted): the run whose four columns lie closest to the row's medians (sum of absolute
  deviations), ties to the lower run number. That is run 1 for all three rows.

## 6. Results

N = 15 per row. Entries are median (min–max).

| Row | N | fsid_blocks | stale_blocks | needle_copies | nonzero_blocks |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| none | 15 | 367 (365–367) | 355 (353–355) | 18 (18–18) | 832 (828–832) |
| async | 15 | 367 (367–367) | 355 (355–355) | 18 (18–18) | 832 (832–832) |
| sync | 15 | 43 (43–43) | 31 (31–31) | 2 (2–2) | 107 (107–107) |

- **Per run.** 44 of 45 images gave exactly the row medians. The exception is run 6, `none`:
  365/353/18/828. It has 2 fewer `sv1` (tree 256) blocks older than generation 30 (EXP-002). So the
  jitter the M2a review saw (one "none" row in 4 repetitions) recurs, here once in 15, and only in
  the no-discard row. Its `needle_copies` stayed 18, where the review's rerun had 16.
- **Wall time** per run (three guests plus three probes): 4.72–6.03 s.
- **Against research.md §10.4:**

  | Row | §10.4 before | Medians now | Differs |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | none | 367/355/18/832 | 367/355/18/832 | no |
  | async | 367/355/18/832 | 367/355/18/832 | no |
  | sync | 43/31/2/107 | 43/31/2/107 | no |

  The table stands; research.md §10.4 now cites these medians and ranges.
- **Effect of `discard=sync`** (medians, sync against none): 11.7 % of the fsid blocks remain
  (43/367), 8.7 % of the stale blocks (31/355, so 91.3 % are gone), 11.1 % of the inline-string
  copies (2/18) and 12.9 % of the non-zero blocks (107/832). The effect (324 stale blocks) is more
  than 150 times the largest observed spread (2 blocks).
- **Async with a quick unmount** equals no discard on every column in all 15 runs.

## 7. Threats to validity

- **Internal.**
  - Guest commit timing shifts a few blocks between runs (run 6). The spread is per column, so
    tolerances come from this table, never from a fixed "± N".
  - Host load was not controlled beyond running guests one at a time. The review's 16
    `needle_copies` was not reproduced; 15 runs cannot bound rare outcomes tightly.
  - All runs used one generator commit and one initramfs; their variation is not measured.
- **External.**
  - Stock Ubuntu 7.0.0-31 guest kernel and host btrfs-progs 6.6.3 only.
  - TRIM lands on a sparse raw file on ext4 through virtio-blk `discard=unmap`: a trimmed range
    reads back as zeros. On an SSD, a trimmed LBA may read as zeros, as old data or
    indeterminately, depending on the drive, and the FTL may keep the flash contents until garbage
    collection. The destruction measured here is what a host sees through a zero-returning block
    layer, not a flash-level statement.
  - One 512 MiB image, one small scenario (38 transactions ending in a full balance), no aging.
  - The async row unmounts about a second after the scenario. Async discard delays its work
    (research.md §10.3), so a guest that stays idle longer can trim as much as sync. That row is
    not measured here (plan.md M7 "async idle").
- **Construct.**
  - `fsid_blocks` and `stale_blocks` count 4 KiB offsets with a matching fsid, without validation:
    invalid mkfs residue and 16 KiB nodes counted once per header are included, and a stale block
    is not necessarily recoverable. EXP-002 measures what btrfska can validate and resolve.
  - `needle_copies` counts bytes, not files; `nonzero_blocks` is a coarse proxy for trimmed space.
  - The "none" row's guest mounts with `discard=async` (§3 caveat), so it measures the absence of
    TRIM at the image, not the absence of the mount option.

## 8. Status

**Supports** the hypothesis: sync keeps 8.7 % of the no-discard stale blocks (below 20 %), async
with a quick unmount equals no discard, and the research.md §10.4 values are the medians of 15
regenerations. Follow-ups:
- a `nodiscard` mount-option row and an async row with ≥ 130 s idle before unmount (plan.md M7);
- larger and aged images, and a real SSD with `discard` passed through;
- the jitter's cause (which commit boundary moves), once M5 timelines can diff the runs.
