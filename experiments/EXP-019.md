# EXP-019 — Free space, the discard mode and the overwrite risk of recovered data, on the corpus

- **Date:** 2026-10-07 (registration, then results)
- **Milestone:** M6b (plan.md "M6b: free-space-tree forensics and overwrite risk"); claim C2
- **Script:** [`experiments/exp019.py`](exp019.py); environment: [`experiments/env.sh`](env.sh)
- **Raw outputs:** `images/scratch/exp/EXP-019/results.jsonl` (gitignored), one line per image
- **Registration:** §1 and §2 were committed before the script was written or run. **What was
  known beforehand**, so the reader can judge how blind this is:
  - while the feature was built, `tests/test_freespace_images.py` ran on the kept corpus images
    (one build per recipe): the free space tree equalled the free space derived from the extent
    tree on all 14 images with a free space tree; the kept `s01` trio was observed as
    `not_trimmed`, `not_trimmed`, `trimmed_metadata`; on the kept `m4_deep`, every regular victim
    had `freed_in.by` equal to the generation of its `delete` event in the log;
  - by hand on the kept images, `recover --root all --tree all --orphans`: on
    `s01_discard_none_r1` the current state's 10 files were `in_use` and every file of a
    pre-balance state `no_block_group`; on `m4_deep` 1 412 artifacts `in_use`, 32 239 `free`,
    4 `partial`, none `allocated`; on `s01_discard_sync_r1` 10 artifacts, all `in_use`.
  Nothing had been measured on fresh builds, and no image other than those named had been
  recovered with the free-space placement.

## 1. Hypothesis

The current state's free space tree, cross-checked against its extent tree, says for every
recovered extent whether its bytes are still held, free or given to another extent; the older
free space trees date the free to one commit; and the image itself shows whether a discard
mode trimmed freed space.

- **H1 (two sources agree).** On every corpus image with a free space tree, kept and fresh, the
  free space tree and the free space derived from the extent tree and block groups hold the same
  free bytes, superblock stripes left out of both (`cross_check` 0 and 0), and no free space
  tree item is skipped or inconsistent.
- **H2 (the discard mode is observable, except async with a quick unmount).** On five fresh
  builds per discard mode of scenario `s01`: every `sync` build is observed as
  `trimmed_metadata`; every `none` and `async` build as `not_trimmed`. Registered because async
  with a quick unmount leaves the image as no discard does (EXP-002), so it cannot be told apart
  from `none` on the image.
- **H3 (a free is dated to its commit).** On five fresh builds of `m4_deep`, every recovered
  regular victim (the even ones: 12 per build) is `free` with `freed_in.by` equal to the
  generation of its `delete` event in the log, and `freed_in.after` below it.
- **H4 (live data is not at risk).** On every image, kept and fresh, every file of the current
  state that read completely is `in_use` with score 0 (the one exception registered: none).
- **H5 (descriptive).** Per image and source kind, artifacts per `space_verdict` and per risk
  level, with the observed discard mode, and again with the mode the guest log's `=== MOUNTED`
  line states (`discard=async` on most images, since the virtio disk advertises discard;
  `discard` on `s01_discard_sync`; none on `m5_reuse*`). No prediction of the counts. One
  qualitative prediction: `allocated` or `partial` appears only on images where space a deleted
  or relocated file used was written again (`m5_reuse`, `m3_wide`, `m4_deep`), and nowhere on
  the `s01` trio.

**What would refute it.** H1: any disagreement means one of the two readings is wrong, or the
kernel's two records drift apart. H2: a `sync` build observed otherwise, or a `none` build
observed as trimmed. H3: any victim dated to another commit. H4: a live file not `in_use`.

**What it means for the paper.** C2 rests on this: no tool reports free-space-tree state today.
H3 is the "prove blocks were freed" half (a free dated to one commit, against ground truth);
H2 and H5 are what the overwrite-risk score can and cannot know from the image alone.

## 2. Method

**Images.**
- *Kept:* every image of `corpus/manifest.tsv` on the host that the tool opens (not
  `m1_unknown_incompat`, refused by the gate). Fixed images: one run each, with the SHA-256
  before and after (plan.md §7, pure-parse results).
- *Fresh:* five builds per mode of `corpus/vm/scenarios/discard_{none,async,sync}.sh` (scenario
  `s01`) and five builds of `m4_deep` (its manifest command), each measured and deleted. Guest
  runs are not bit-stable, so every count is given as median and range over the five.

**Per image.** `catalog build --full-sweep`; then, from the database and the image:
- the current allocation read both ways (`SpaceViews` with and without the free space tree):
  source, `cross_check`, the findings on the free space tree items, the older free space trees
  read;
- the observed discard mode and its evidence;
- `recover --root all --tree all --orphans` with the observed mode, and again with the mode the
  log's `=== MOUNTED` line states (`--discard`); per source kind, artifacts per `space_verdict`
  and per risk level;
- H4: the current state's complete files and their verdicts and scores;
- H3 (`m4_deep`): per regular victim, `freed_in` against the log's `delete` generation.

**Success.** H1 to H4 as stated in §1, with no tolerance: each is a yes or no per image.

## 7. Threats to validity

- **Internal.** The observed mode rests on the scan's `zeroed` walk failures and on orphan tree
  blocks in free space; on these images the two never occur together. An image where a FITRIM
  ran after metadata was freed also reads `trimmed_metadata`, which is why the mode is named for
  what is seen, not for a mount option. The data evidence sampled nothing on the `s01` builds:
  no freed data extent there is named by a leaf of the current chunk map's time (the balance
  gives every chunk a new map), so `trimmed_data` was never exercised.
- **External.** Virtio-blk TRIM on a sparse raw file reads back as zeros; SSDs differ (DRAT,
  RZAT; research.md §10.3). Async discard was measured with a quick unmount only, so the
  `async` rule of the score (data-only block groups trimmed 120 s after the next mount) is read
  from the source, not measured. No image lacks a free space tree: the derivation from the
  extent tree was checked against the tree on every image (H1), not on a filesystem with a space
  cache v1, which the corpus does not hold.
- **Construct.** The score is ordinal, built from kernel behaviour: which bytes the next
  allocation, discard or block-group deletion can take first, not a probability. `allocated`
  says the space belongs to another extent in the current state; a prealloc extent holds space
  without writing it, so `allocated` bytes may still be the old ones (the data checksum of M6a
  says whether they are).

## 8. Status

- **H1 supported:** on the 23 opened kept images and the 20 fresh builds the two sources agree
  to the byte (superblock stripes left out of both), and no free space tree item, current or
  older, was skipped or inconsistent.
- **H2 supported:** `sync` 5/5 `trimmed_metadata`; `none` 5/5 and `async` 5/5 `not_trimmed`.
- **H3 supported, with a wrong count in the registration:** every recovered regular victim is
  freed in the commit the log names, on all seven `m4_deep` images (two kept, five fresh). The
  registration said 12 per build; 10 are recovered, because no cataloged state holds victims 2
  and 4 (the oldest states do not survive), so they are neither recovered nor dated.
- **H4 supported:** every complete file of the current state, on every image, is `in_use`
  with score 0.
- **H5:** the qualitative prediction was partly wrong. Bytes given to another extent appear on
  `m5_reuse` and also on its control `m5_reuse_same`, whose next chunk is laid on the removed
  chunk's own physical bytes, so the old files' bytes were written again there too. None appear
  on `m3_wide` or `m4_deep`, where no recovered extent's space was taken by another extent. None
  on the `s01` trio, as predicted.
- Follow-ups: a corpus image with a space cache v1 (no free space tree) to exercise the
  fallback on a real filesystem; an `async` build that stays mounted for more than 120 s
  (research.md §10.6) to exercise `trimmed_data`.
