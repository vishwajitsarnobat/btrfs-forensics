# EXP-017 — A superblock copy of another filesystem at a mirror offset is reported, not selected

- **Date:** 2026-10-07
- **Milestone:** paper readiness, gap G13 (`docs/paper-draft.md`); the measurement behind N3, F10
  and traceability row 7 (catalog.md, M1a review fix 1)
- **Script:** [`experiments/exp017.py`](exp017.py); environment: [`experiments/env.sh`](env.sh)
- **Raw outputs:** `images/scratch/exp/EXP-017/results.json` (gitignored)
- **Registration:** not blind. The behaviour was built and tested in the M1a review
  (`tests/test_superblock.py`, `tests/test_vm_images.py`); this record measures it as a table over
  the whole corpus. The prediction written before the run: on `m1_foreign_mirror` btrfska selects
  mirror 0 and reports mirror 1 as foreign while generation alone picks mirror 1; on every other
  image the rules agree and nothing is foreign.

## 1. Hypothesis

A valid superblock copy of a different filesystem can sit at a mirror offset (residue of an
earlier `mkfs`), and a selection by generation alone picks it whenever its generation is higher.
btrfska anchors the filesystem identity on the lowest-offset valid copy, as btrfs-progs' recover
mode does (`kernel-shared/disk-io.c:2022-2064` at btrfs-progs v7.1), so it does not select such a
copy and reports it instead. Registered: on `m1_foreign_mirror`, btrfska selects the primary,
reports exactly one foreign copy, and generation alone would pick that foreign copy; on the other
20 images (the control) the two rules pick the same copy and no copy is foreign.

**What would refute it.** btrfska selecting the foreign copy or not reporting it; generation alone
picking the primary (then the image does not test what it claims); a foreign copy or a
disagreement on any control image.

**What it means for the paper.** N3 and F10: a foreign copy is evidence of a previous filesystem
on the device, and a reader that selects superblocks by generation alone would take its identity
and roots for this filesystem's.

## 2. Method

For every image, every superblock mirror slot is read (`superblock.read_copies`; mirror 2 at
256 GiB lies beyond these images). Three rules are applied to the valid copies:
- **btrfska** (`superblock.select`): the lowest-offset valid copy anchors the identity (fsid, and
  metadata_uuid when its METADATA_UUID bit is set); only copies of that filesystem compete on
  generation; the others are listed as foreign and reported;
- **generation alone**: the valid copy with the highest generation, whatever its fsid (the rule
  `select()` used before the M1a review fix);
- **the kernel's**: mirror 0 only (`fs/btrfs/disk-io.c:3333` at v7.0).

Where generation alone picks a different copy than btrfska, the first two steps of
`fs.open_filesystem` (the bootstrap chunk map from that copy's sys_chunk_array, then the read of
its chunk root) are replayed with that copy's fields, and with the selected copy's for
comparison. Each image's SHA-256 is taken before and after.

## 6. Results

N = 1 (deterministic), 21 images.

| Image | Valid copies | btrfska selects | Foreign copies reported | Generation alone picks | Kernel mounts |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `m1_foreign_mirror` | 2 | mirror 0 (generation 38, fsid `a74d6f92…`, xxhash64) | 1: mirror 1 | **mirror 1** (generation 1000, fsid `face3a8e…`, sha256) | mirror 0 |
| `m1_mirror_damage` (control) | 1 | mirror 1 (generation 38) | 0 | mirror 1 | mirror 0, invalid |
| the other 19 images (control) | 2 | mirror 0 | 0 | mirror 0 | mirror 0 |

- **On `m1_foreign_mirror`** btrfska reports
  `mirror 1 foreign superblock at 67108864 (fsid face3a8e-30da-426c-b2e9-f0ee32027eff, generation 1000)`
  and selects mirror 0, as the kernel would; generation alone picks mirror 1.
- **What the wrong pick would read.** The foreign copy names chunk root 131121152, which is also
  this filesystem's chunk root address (both images come from the same scenario). Read with the
  foreign copy's context the block fails on both DUP copies: its checksum does not verify as
  sha256 (the block carries an xxhash64 checksum), and its header fsid `a74d6f92…` is not the
  foreign `face3a8e…`. With the selected copy the same block is valid. On this image, then, a
  generation-only reader would fail to open the filesystem rather than misread it.
- **Control.** On the other 20 images the rules agree and no copy is foreign. On
  `m1_mirror_damage` (zeroed primary) btrfska and generation alone both fall back to mirror 1,
  where the kernel would refuse.
- **The result equals the documents.** paper-draft.md N3, F10 and row 7 and catalog.md M1a review
  fix 1 say the foreign copy is reported and not selected and would win a generation-only
  selection; all three hold. catalog.md's fsid `55692877…` for the foreign copy is that of the
  earlier build of `m1_sha256_bgt`; the fsid changes with every build.

## 7. Threats to validity

- **Internal.** One synthetic image, one foreign copy, planted at mirror 1 with an inflated
  generation (1000). The selection rule does not depend on the generation value once it exceeds
  the primary's, and the synthetic cases of `tests/test_superblock.py` cover a damaged primary,
  metadata_uuid anchoring with and without the feature bit, and the CLI marker.
- **External.** Not observed on a real reformatted device. There, the foreign copy's trees may
  survive too (they would on a device reformatted without discard), and a generation-only reader
  could then read the old filesystem's trees instead of failing: **UNVERIFIED**, it needs an image
  with a real previous filesystem (M6 foreign-fsid discovery, M7 corpus).
- **Construct.** "Generation alone" is the rule btrfska itself used before the M1a fix; whether a
  given third-party tool selects that way is not measured here.

## 8. Status

**Supports** the hypothesis. What the paper may say: on a synthetic image with a valid superblock
copy of another filesystem at mirror 1, btrfska selects the primary and reports the foreign copy
(btrfs-progs recover-mode rule), whereas a selection by generation alone would pick the foreign
copy; on this image that wrong pick fails at the chunk root. On 20 control images nothing is
reported as foreign. Follow-up: a real reformatted device.
