# EXP-014 — Single-bit flips of a compressed LZO sector decode to wrong bytes without an error

- **Date:** 2026-10-07
- **Milestone:** paper readiness, gap G13 (`docs/paper-draft.md`); the measurement behind plan.md
  §3.5 ("Also observed"), the plan.md §9 risk row on LZO, and paper-draft N4 and F9
- **Script:** [`experiments/exp014.py`](exp014.py), which draws its streams with
  [`tests/oracle/lzo_hostile.py`](../tests/oracle/lzo_hostile.py); environment:
  [`experiments/env.sh`](env.sh)
- **Raw outputs:** `images/scratch/exp/EXP-014/results.json` (gitignored)
- **Registration:** not blind. The numbers were first produced by the harness in the M1c review
  (catalog.md, "LZO harness results"), and this record re-measures them with a script that reports
  them as a table. The prediction written before the run was that the counts of that table would
  come back unchanged, because they are a deterministic function of the seed and the decoder
  versions.

## 1. Hypothesis

LZO1X carries no integrity check, so flipping one bit of a compressed 4 KiB sector usually yields a
stream that every correct decoder accepts and turns into wrong bytes of plausible length.
Registered: per seed (1–5), most of the 300 flipped streams (more than half) decode without an
error to bytes other than the original sector in btrfska, in lzallright and in dissect.util's
native decoder, and the three return the same bytes on every such stream.

**What would refute it.** Fewer than half of the flipped streams decoding without an error in any of
the three; or the three decoders disagreeing on a stream, which would mean that one of them does
not implement LZO1X as the others do (then "wrong bytes" could be a decoder bug, not a property of
the format).

**What it means for the paper.** Decode success is not evidence of correct content (N4, F9): a
recovered compressed extent is Confirmed only by a data checksum (M6), never by decoding cleanly.

## 2. Method

Per seed, exactly as `lzo_hostile.run` draws them (the script checks this against the harness in
`tests/test_exp014.py`): a `random.Random(seed)` draws 2 000 round-trip vectors (discarded here;
they only advance the generator), then a 4 KiB sector of bytes from `abcdefgh\n ` (low-entropy
text), which lzallright compresses once, then 300 copies of that stream with one bit flipped at a
uniformly random bit position.

Each copy is decoded with an output bound of 4 KiB by btrfska (`btrfska.substrate.lzo`),
lzallright 0.2.6 (lzokay), dissect.util 3.24's pure-Python decoder and its native (Rust) decoder.
An outcome is `correct` (the original sector), `wrong` (other bytes, at most 4 KiB, no error),
`> 4 KiB` (more than the bound returned: the bound is only a size hint to that decoder), a
catchable `Exception`, or a `BaseException` that is not an `Exception` (pyo3's `PanicException`,
which `except Exception` does not catch). Also counted: wrong outputs exactly one sector long
(the corruption does not even change the size), the streams all three of btrfska, lzallright and
dissect.util native decode to wrong bytes, and on how many of those the three outputs are
identical. Every decoder must first decode the unflipped stream to the sector.

## 3. Image / scenario

No disk image: the inputs are generated from the seeds. Decoder versions are fixed by `uv.lock`
(sha256 below): lzallright 0.2.6, dissect.util 3.24 (with its native module), dissect.btrfs 1.10
(not used here). The counts are a deterministic function of the seed, Python's `random` module
and these versions; one run is the measurement.

## 6. Results

N = 5 seeds, 300 flipped streams per seed; compressed sector 2 863, 2 856, 2 841, 2 820 and 2 831
bytes. Every decoder decoded every unflipped stream to the sector.

**Wrong bytes within 4 KiB, without an error, per seed:**

| Decoder | Seed 1 | Seed 2 | Seed 3 | Seed 4 | Seed 5 | Median (range) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| btrfska | 227 | 231 | 229 | 218 | 219 | 227 (218–231) |
| lzallright 0.2.6 | 227 | 231 | 229 | 218 | 219 | 227 (218–231) |
| dissect.util 3.24 pure Python | 258 | 277 | 267 | 271 | 258 | 267 (258–277) |
| dissect.util 3.24 native | 227 | 231 | 229 | 218 | 219 | 227 (218–231) |

**All outcomes, median (range) over the seeds:**

| Decoder | Correct | Wrong ≤ 4 KiB | > 4 KiB | `Exception` | non-`Exception` | Wrong and exactly 4 KiB | Failure types |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| btrfska | 0 (0–1) | 227 (218–231) | 0 (0–0) | 72 (68–82) | 0 (0–0) | 209 (196–210) | `LzoError` |
| lzallright 0.2.6 | 0 (0–1) | 227 (218–231) | 36 (32–41) | 39 (31–47) | 0 (0–0) | 209 (196–210) | `LZOError` |
| dissect.util 3.24 pure Python | 1 (0–1) | 267 (258–277) | 30 (22–41) | 1 (0–2) | 0 (0–0) | 210 (204–225) | `IndexError`, `ValueError` |
| dissect.util 3.24 native | 0 (0–1) | 227 (218–231) | 36 (32–41) | 0 (0–2) | 37 (31–46) | 209 (196–210) | `PanicException`, `ValueError` |

**Across decoders.** In every seed, btrfska, lzallright and dissect.util native decode exactly the
same streams to wrong bytes (227, 231, 229, 218, 219), and on every one of them the three return
identical bytes.

- **The hypothesis holds.** 218–231 of 300 flipped streams per seed (73–77 %) decode without an
  error to wrong bytes in the three decoders that implement LZO1X's bounds, identically.
- **Most of the corruption is silent in size too.** 196–210 of those wrong outputs per seed are
  exactly 4 KiB long, like the original sector.
- **The numbers equal the documents.** plan.md §3.5, the plan.md §9 risk row, catalog.md (M1c
  review fixes) and paper-draft.md (§6.5, N4, F9) give 227 (218–231) for the three decoders,
  258–277 for the pure-Python one, 22–41 over the bound and 31–46 panics; all reproduce exactly.
- One flip per seed in seeds 1 and 2 (and one more in seed 3 for the pure-Python decoder) is
  `correct`: a flip that changes nothing the decoder reads.
- btrfska never returns more than its bound and never fails with anything but `LzoError`;
  lzallright and both dissect.util decoders return more than 4 KiB for 22–41 streams, and the
  native decoder panics with a non-`Exception` on 31–46.

## 7. Threats to validity

- **Internal.** One sector content per seed, low-entropy text compressed by one encoder
  (lzallright). The share of flips that decode depends on the share of the stream that is literal
  bytes versus instructions: a flip in a literal byte never changes how the stream parses, so it
  always decodes (to one wrong byte, or more where a later match copies it). Data that
  compresses better (more instructions, fewer literals) would give fewer silent decodes, and
  incompressible data more.
- **External.** Real btrfs LZO extents wrap LZO1X segments in the btrfs framing (plan.md §3.5); a
  flip in a segment or total length header is not covered here (btrfska's framing code checks
  those lengths separately).
  The kernel's encoder is not lzallright's; the stream layout is the same format, but the
  instruction mix may differ.
- **Construct.** "Wrong bytes" means "not the original sector", which is what an examiner cares
  about; it does not measure how many bytes differ. The agreement of three independent
  implementations is what licenses calling the outputs a property of the format rather than of
  btrfska.

## 8. Status

**Supports** the hypothesis. What the paper may say: about three quarters of single-bit flips of a
compressed 4 KiB LZO sector decode without an error to wrong bytes, identically in btrfska,
lzallright and dissect.util's native decoder; decode success is therefore never evidence of
correct content. Follow-ups: other contents (incompressible, highly repetitive) and flips in the
btrfs framing; the same harness against the kernel's decoder in the guest (UNVERIFIED that it
behaves as lzokay does on every stream, although both follow `lzo1x_decompress_safe`'s checks).
