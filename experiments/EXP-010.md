# EXP-010 — Logical-range reuse: per-generation chunk maps against one merged map

- **Date:** 2026-10-07 (registration, committed as `3bf711f` and pushed before measuring; results the same day)
- **Milestone:** M5f (plan.md), issue #57; claim C6
- **Script:** [`experiments/exp010.py`](exp010.py); environment: [`experiments/env.sh`](env.sh)
- **Raw outputs:** `images/scratch/exp/EXP-010/results.jsonl` (gitignored), one line per build
- **Registration:** §1 to §3 and §5 were committed before any file of a `reuse` or `reuse_same`
  image was read through any chunk map. **What was known beforehand**, so the reader can judge
  how blind this is:
  - the answer on issue #48: the kernel rules for where a new chunk starts, and that no corpus
    image has a logical range held by two chunks;
  - four pilot builds made while writing the scenarios, of which only the serial logs (chunk,
    block-group and device-extent items, as `btrfs inspect-internal dump-tree` prints them in the
    guest) were read. The first, the layout issue #48 proposed, put the new chunk back on the
    removed chunk's own bytes, not on the lower hole the answer predicted; this is how the
    largest-hole rule of §2.1 was found, and that layout became the control. The second and
    third, with the six-chunk layout of `reuse`, gave the layout §1 predicts; the fourth gave it
    for `reuse_same`;
  - for the third pilot only, `btrfska catalog build --full-sweep` and a listing of `chunks` and
    `stripes`: the historical map of generation 16 places the topmost data chunk at logical
    399507456 on physical 441450496, the current map places 399507456 on 173015040, and the
    `dev_extents` map rejects 399507456 with two device extents.
  No file, extent or block was read through any map on any of these images, by either mode.

## 1. Hypothesis

Where one logical range belonged to two chunks with different physical placement, a file version
of the older chunk's time reads back hash-exact through the chunk map of its own time and wrong
through a map merged from every CHUNK_ITEM with the newest record per chunk start winning; where
the placement did not change, the two readings are the same.

Definitions. *Layout holds* on a `reuse` image when the current map has a data chunk R whose
logical start equals that of a data chunk B of an older map, R's stripe differs from B's, and
B's device range lies in no chunk of the current map. On a `reuse_same` image it holds when R and
B have the same logical start, length and stripe and some map between the two does not place
that address. A *full version* of a file is a file artifact whose size is the size the guest
wrote; the log holds its SHA-256. A *prefix version* is one of a smaller size, from a commit in
the middle of writing (every file is written 4 MiB at a time).

- **H0 (the scenarios).** Layout holds in at least 4 of 5 builds of each scenario.
- **H1 (the effect, `reuse`).** In every build where the layout holds, every full version of
  `b.bin` is `complete` with the logged SHA-256 under `--maps own`, and `complete` with another
  SHA-256 under the merged map (every extent is placed, so nothing tells the reader that the
  bytes are someone else's). Under `--maps current` it is the same as under the merged map.
  Registered: all full versions of `b.bin`, in every build where the layout holds.
- **H2 (the warning, `reuse`).** Every full version of `b.bin` read under `--maps own` carries
  the note that a newer map gives the address to a different chunk.
- **H3 (where the two agree, `reuse`).** Every file artifact that is not a version of `b.bin`
  has the same status and SHA-256 under `--maps own` and under the merged map. That includes
  `z.bin`, whose device bytes R now holds: its full version reads `complete` with a SHA-256 other
  than the logged one in both (c.bin's first pieces overwrote its first pieces), and under
  `--maps own` it carries the note that a newer map allocated the space again.
- **H4 (the control, `reuse_same`).** Every file artifact has the same status and SHA-256 under
  `--maps own` and under the merged map. The full version of `b.bin` is `complete` with a
  SHA-256 other than the logged one in both (c.bin overwrote the start of its part in B), and it
  carries no note about reuse: the maps cannot see a reuse that kept the placement; only data
  checksums (M6) can.

**What would refute it.** A full version of `b.bin` that is not hash-exact under `--maps own` on
a `reuse` image where the layout holds (then the per-generation reading is wrong, or the bytes
were overwritten, and the experiment must find out which). One that is hash-exact under the
merged map (then the merged map did not translate the range as predicted). Any artifact outside
`b.bin` that differs between the two readings (then the two designs differ in more than the
reused range, and the explanation is owed).

**What it means for the paper.** H1 is the case C6 rests on against `mbkn-btrfs-rescue`:
per-generation maps read what a merged newest-wins map misreads. H3 and H4 bound it: the
difference is confined to a reused range with changed placement, and with unchanged placement
neither design notices an overwrite. If H0 fails, the case is rarer than the kernel rules say,
and C6's wording must say so.

## 2. Method

### 2.1 How a new data chunk is placed (found in the first pilot)

The answer on issue #48 assumed that a new chunk takes the lowest device hole that fits. At
v7.0 a data chunk asks `find_free_dev_extent` for `max_stripe_size * dev_stripes`
(`gather_device_info`, `fs/btrfs/volumes.c:5529`), with `max_stripe_size` = min(chunk_size,
1 GiB) (`volumes.c:5449`) and a data `chunk_size` of `BTRFS_MAX_DATA_CHUNK_SIZE`
(`space-info.c:216-217`). The search returns the first hole of that size, and when there is
none, the largest hole (`volumes.c:1883-1946`); the chunk is then cut to 10 % of the writable
space (`volumes.c:5454-5456`). On a 512 MiB device no hole is 1 GiB, so a new data chunk always
starts at the largest hole. The topmost chunk is also the last one on the device, so when it is
removed its hole joins the free space behind it; the new chunk lands elsewhere only if a lower
hole is larger.

### 2.2 Scenarios

Both in `corpus/vm/scenarios/`, pinned `mkfs.btrfs` 6.6.3, guest kernel 7.0.0-31, 512 MiB,
xxhash, `commit=5,nodiscard`, no compression. Every file is written in 4 MiB pieces from
`/dev/urandom`, each fsynced, so every data extent is 4 MiB and a 64 MiB chunk holds 16 pieces.

- **`reuse`:** `keep.txt`; `a.bin` (16 pieces, chunk D1), `z.bin` (32, D2 and D3), `w.bin` (32,
  D4 and D5), `b.bin` (10, D6 = B); SHA-256 of all five logged; `rm z.bin b.bin`; `btrfs balance
  start -dusage=0` (removes D6, D3, D2); commit; `c.bin` = the free pieces of the surviving data
  chunks + 4, so that one new chunk R is needed. Expected: R at B's logical address, on D2's
  physical offset (the 128 MiB hole of D2 and D3 beats the 91 MiB of D6 and the tail).
- **`reuse_same`:** `keep.txt`; `z.bin` (20 pieces, chunk Z and the start of W), `w.bin` (5, W),
  `b.bin` (14, the rest of W and the start of B); logged; `rm z.bin b.bin`; `balance -dusage=0`
  (removes B, then Z); commit; `c.bin` as above. Expected: R at B's logical address and on B's
  physical offset (B's hole and the tail beat Z's 64 MiB).

### 2.3 Readings

Per build: `catalog build --full-sweep`; then `recover --root all --tree all` three times, into
separate directories:
- **own:** `--maps own`, btrfska's reading (M5a): each state through its own map, then the newer
  maps, then the `dev_extents` map;
- **current:** `--maps current`, the current map only;
- **merged (emulation):** one map built by `exp010.py` from the accepted chunks of every
  `historical` map and the `current` map in the evidence database: for each chunk start, the
  chunk of the map with the newest chunk-root generation wins (the current map is newest). Every
  cataloged state is read through it, by the engine's own `recover_roots`. This emulates the
  design research.md §12 describes for `mbkn-btrfs-rescue` (`model.py` `_load_chunks`, newest
  record per chunk start; `map_logical`, a bisection on chunk start); it is not that tool's code,
  and it is fed by btrfska's catalog, not by that tool's scan. A lookup takes the chunk with the
  greatest start not above the address, as a bisection on start does, even where chunks of
  different starts overlap.

Artifacts are matched across the three runs by (state, tree, inode, creation generation). A
duplicate is resolved to the artifact that holds its content. For each scenario the script
counts, per file name of the log: full versions; of them `complete` with the logged SHA-256 in
each reading; `complete` with another SHA-256; `partial`; carrying the reuse note ("places this
address in a different chunk") or the reallocation note ("the space was allocated again") under
own. Prefix versions are checked against the full version read under own: a prefix version of
size k is right if its SHA-256 is that of the first k bytes of the full version (counted
separately; a prefix is never compared with the log). And over all other file artifacts: how
many differ between own and merged in status or SHA-256. The layout check of §1 is computed from
`chunks` and `stripes`. The image's hash is taken before and after.

**Reported.** For every quantity the median and the range over five builds per scenario, and the
host. The two corpus images (`m5_reuse`, `m5_reuse_same`) are measured as extra lines.

### 6.1 What the numbers say

- **H0 holds, 5 of 5 for both scenarios**, and with the same addresses in every build: the
  largest-hole rule of §2.1 decides the placement deterministically on this image size.
- **H1 holds.** On `reuse`, both full versions of `b.bin` read hash-exact through the map of
  their own time, and both read `complete` with a wrong SHA-256 through the merged map: every
  extent is placed, so the merged reading gives no sign that the bytes belong to `c.bin` and the
  old `z.bin`. The current map reads the same wrong bytes. The prefix version of `b.bin` (from a
  commit while it was being written) is right under own and wrong under merged, as its last
  piece lies in B.
- **H2 holds for the reads**, with a qualification about duplicates (6.2).
- **H3 holds.** Outside `b.bin`, the merged map and the per-generation maps agree on every
  artifact, in every build. `z.bin`, whose device bytes R took, is wrong in both, and the own
  reading says so with the reallocation note on every full version. Under `--maps current` it is
  `partial`, because no current chunk holds D2 or D3 any more.
- **H4 holds.** On `reuse_same` the two readings agree on every artifact; the full version of
  `b.bin` is `complete` with a wrong SHA-256 in all three, and no note warns of it.

### 6.2 What was not predicted

- **Duplicates do not repeat the note.** The second full version of `b.bin` has the same content
  as the first, so recovery records it as a `duplicate` that points at the first; the note is on
  the first artifact's row only. §2 had registered resolving duplicates to the artifact that holds
  the content, so H2 counts the resolved row; the raw rows carry the note once. A reader who
  filters artifacts by their own `problems` misses it on duplicates. `z.bin` shows the same (7
  resolved, 1 on its own row).
- **`z.bin` in the control is lost too, and silently.** Its last four pieces lay in W, the chunk
  that survives; `c.bin` refilled W's freed space before R was created, so those pieces were
  overwritten inside a chunk that never changed. No map can see that (EXP-007 §7); only data
  checksums can. The control's `z.bin` is `complete` with a wrong SHA-256 under own and merged,
  with no note.
- **The layout proposed on issue #48 does not give different placement** (registration, first
  pilot); §2.1 is a correction to that answer: on a device with no 1 GiB hole a new data chunk
  takes the largest hole, not the first that fits.

## 7. Threats to validity

- **Internal.** The scenario is built to produce the case: every file is written in 4 MiB
  pieces, fsynced one by one, and the removal is forced with `balance -dusage=0`. That the
  layout appears 5 of 5 shows the kernel rules are deterministic here, not that the case is
  common. The truth for the reading is the guest's SHA-256 of the whole file; prefix versions
  are checked only against a full version read under own.
- **Emulation.** The merged map is built from btrfska's catalog, from the chunk maps of
  surviving chunk-tree roots, not from every CHUNK_ITEM in every leaf as `mbkn-btrfs-rescue`
  stores them, and its rules follow research.md §12's reading of that tool's source. Where both
  hold B and R at one start, the newest wins either way, so the result for the reused range does
  not depend on which leaves were found; it was not checked against the tool itself, which is
  M7's baseline work.
- **External.** One device, 512 MiB, SINGLE data, no compression, stock 7.0 guest, virtio, no
  discard. On a device with a free hole of 1 GiB or more the first fitting hole is taken
  instead (§2.1), and the new chunk lands on the removed chunk's own bytes only if that is the
  lowest such hole; larger images were not built. Multi-device and striped profiles, where the
  placement rule sorts devices by free space, were not tested.
- **Construct.** "Hash-exact" is checked against the scenario's log, which exists only in the
  lab; on evidence the reader has the reuse note (H2) and, after M6, data checksums.

## 8. Status

**Supports H0 to H4** (N = 5 per scenario, no spread). Per-generation maps read the version of a
file in a reused logical range hash-exact where a merged newest-wins map reads other bytes
without any sign of it; outside such a range the two readings agree, and with unchanged
placement neither notices an overwrite. Follow-ups: copy the reuse note onto duplicate
artifacts, or document that a duplicate's notes are its original's; data-checksum verification
(M6) for the overwrite cases of 6.2; the same scenario on an image with a 1 GiB hole; the
comparison against `mbkn-btrfs-rescue` itself (M7).
