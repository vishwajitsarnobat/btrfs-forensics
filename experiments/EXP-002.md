- **Raw outputs:** `images/scratch/exp/EXP-002/` (gitignored): `results.jsonl` (one line per image:
  probe columns, the compatibility count and its skip-range diff, btrfska's classes, walk failures,

## 1. Hypothesis

- **Pre-registered** (plan.md M2): on every discard-trio image, btrfska's full-sweep scan,
  counted under `probe_stale_metadata.py`'s own rules, gives exactly the probe's `fsid_blocks` and
  `stale_blocks`. This is a deterministic parse, so no tolerance applies. Equal counts are
  *coverage agreement* (§2), not an independent re-implementation of the probe.
- **Exploratory** (no prediction was registered before the measurement): how many candidate
  root-tree blocks (states), and how much of each, old-root discovery still finds under each
  discard mode. These results are reported as observations.

## 2. Method

**Images.** The 45 images of EXP-000 (runs 1–15 × none, async, sync), measured before the
cleanup, and, for agreement only, the three earlier `images/scenarios/s01_discard_{none,async,sync}.img`
(generated on 2026-09-15 before M2b by the same scripts, not in the manifest). Every image was
measured once, sequentially, with one worker.

**Agreement.** For each image:
1. `probe_stale_metadata.py IMAGE`, unchanged, as a subprocess;
2. `probe_compat(IMAGE)`: the probe's first two columns computed from btrfska.

First, the ranges each tool reads were diffed. `probe_compat` derives them from the scan plan on
every image rather than assuming them. On a 512 MiB image (131 072 blocks of 4 KiB):

| Block range (4 KiB) | Bytes | Probe | btrfska `--full-sweep` | Why btrfska differs |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0–15 | 0x0–0xFFFF | probed | skipped (`reserved`) | No tree block can lie there. On a regular device the kernel allocates device extents only from `BTRFS_DEVICE_RANGE_RESERVED`, 1 MiB (v7.0 fs.h:104-108; volumes.c:1664-1671 `dev_extent_search_start`; volumes.c:8257-8266 warns when an older mkfs placed one lower), and btrfs-progs mkfs places its first chunk at 1 MiB (v6.6.3 kernel-shared/ctree.h:207, mkfs/common.c:388). `exclude_super_stripes` (block-group.c:2277-2330) is a guarantee about *logical* addresses below 64 KiB and the superblock copies, not about physical offsets |
| 16 | 0x10000–0x10FFF | skipped (primary superblock) | skipped (`reserved`) | — |
| 16384 | 64 MiB | skipped (mirror 1) | skipped (`superblock`) | — |
| 67108864 | 256 GiB | beyond the image | beyond the image | — |
| every other block | | probed | probed (full sweep: chunk stripes, DATA included, and unmapped gaps) | — |

So blocks 0–15 are the only blocks one tool reads and the other does not: 16 blocks, probe only.
No block is read by btrfska only.

**Compatibility count** (`experiments/exp002.py`, `probe_compat`):
- `fsid_blocks` = every btrfska full-sweep candidate (every 4 KiB offset whose 16 bytes at +0x20
  equal the tree fsid; invalid candidates included) + the blocks among 0–15 whose bytes at +0x20
  equal the primary superblock's fsid;
- `stale_blocks` = the candidates whose header generation (+0x50) is below the **primary**
  superblock's generation, valid or not + the same test on those boot-area hits;
- the two remaining definitional differences are checked per image, not assumed: the probe's fsid
  (superblock `fsid`) equals btrfska's tree fsid (no METADATA_UUID), and the primary superblock's
  generation equals the selected copy's. Both held on all 48 images;
- sectorsize is 4096 on every image, so btrfska probes the same offsets as the probe.

Nothing else was adjusted. Columns 3 and 4 (inline-string copies, non-zero blocks) are not tree-block
counts and are reported from the probe only.

**What equal counts show: coverage agreement.** The compatibility count is computed from
btrfska's own scan plan and FSID prefilter (`iter_candidate_nodes`), so equal counts verify that:
- btrfska's regions cover every offset the probe reads outside blocks 0–15;
- its prefilter matches the same 16 bytes at +0x20;
- it reads the generation from the same field, +0x50.

It is not an independent re-implementation of the probe's count, and it does not validate
btrfska's validity checks, reachability classes or discovery.

**btrfska's own classes** (`scan_image`, full sweep, as `btrfska scan --full-sweep`): candidates,
valid, invalid, `live`, `backup_reachable`, `unreferenced`, valid orphans outside the current
chunk map, and legacy-compatible orphans. Walk failures are counted by class (README, `btrfska
roots`).

**Old-root discovery** (`discover_image`, full sweep, as `btrfska roots --full-sweep`). Definitions
(README, `btrfska roots`):
- a **candidate root** is a valid scanned block that no indexed internal block one level up, of
  an acceptable owner and of the block's generation or a newer one, points to, at any level;
- a **candidate root-tree block (state)** is an owner-1 candidate root. It stands for the root tree
  as far as that block reaches and the trees its ROOT_ITEMs name, resolved through the scanned
  blocks. It is evidence of one root tree, not proof of a whole committed filesystem state;
- **completeness** is found / referenced distinct tree blocks of that root tree and of every tree
  a ROOT_ITEM in its found leaves names, except ROOT_ITEMs naming tree 1. The chunk tree and the
  log tree are excluded: no ROOT_ITEM names them. With nothing missing, completeness 1 means that
  every block of the root tree and of every ROOT_ITEM-named tree was found, nothing more;
- a state is *beyond the backups* when neither the superblock nor a backup slot names its
  root-tree block.

Measured per image: root-tree candidate roots; the superblock and backup slot references
rediscovered (indexed, and candidate roots), and from the review re-measurement on, the distinct
blocks they name; every evaluated state's completeness.

**Statistics.** Per image all counts are deterministic. Across runs, per mode: median (min–max),
N = 15.

## 6. Results

### 6.1 Coverage agreement with the probe (pre-registered)

**48 of 48 images give equal counts** on both columns: the 45 regenerated images and the 3 earlier
ones. This is coverage agreement as defined in §2.

| Mode | Images | Probe fsid_blocks / stale_blocks | Compatibility count | Equal | Boot-area (blocks 0–15) fsid hits |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| none | 15 + 1 earlier | 367/355 on 15 images, 365/353 on run 6 | identical per image | 16/16 | 0 |
| async | 15 + 1 earlier | 367/355 on all | identical per image | 16/16 | 0 |
| sync | 15 + 1 earlier | 43/31 on all | identical per image | 16/16 | 0 |

- The boot-area supplement was 0 on every image: blocks 0–15 hold no fsid match on any of the 48
  images, and they are all zero bytes on the representative images. So on this corpus the
  compatibility count equals btrfska's plain full-sweep candidate count and its "generation below
  the superblock" count. The reconciliation still matters in general: an image with a foreign or
  planted fsid in the first 64 KiB would count there for the probe only.
- **Block by block**, the legitimate differences between the probe and btrfska's own classes are:
  - the 5 invalid candidates on every image are mkfs generation-1 blocks at 1081344, 1097728,
    1114112, 1130496 and 1146880 (owners 3, 4, 5, 7, 10). All five lack the WRITTEN flag, and the
    tree-5 leaf is also empty (research.md §10.10). The probe counts them as fsid and stale blocks;
    btrfska reports them as `invalid`, never as orphans;
  - "stale" (generation below the superblock) is not "orphan". On the none/async images, 355 stale =
    350 valid stale copies + the 5 invalid ones; the 350 valid ones classify as 10 `live` (blocks
    unchanged since an older generation, such as fs root generation 19), 24 `backup_reachable` and
    316 `unreferenced`. Of the 12 non-stale (generation 38) copies, 12 are `live`. On sync:
    31 = 26 valid + 5 invalid, of which 10 `live` and 16 `unreferenced`.

**Slot references are not distinct blocks.** The superblock (root and chunk) and the four backup
slots (six trees each) make 26 slot references, but they name only 14 distinct blocks: all four
backups share the fs root (generation 19) and the csum root (generation 35), backups 35–37 share
one chunk root (131104768) and one dev root (64847872), and the superblock's roots are backup 38's.
The N = 15 runs recorded slot references only; the distinct-block counts come from the review
re-measurement of the three kept images (§6.6): 14 of 14 distinct blocks indexed and candidate
roots under none and async, **6 of 14 under sync**.

The same result on every image of a mode. On the representative images:

- **none and async.** 35 candidate root-tree blocks (states):
  - the 4 backup-root states (generations 35–38, generation 38 also current), each complete (10
    of 10 blocks);
  - **31 states beyond the backups**: generations 3, 6, 7 (two root-tree blocks), and 8–34 (the full
    range, one per transaction); 30 of them have every referenced block found (8–13 blocks each).
    Every one is a level-0 root-tree block, so each is a whole root tree; none is level-inconsistent
    and no root-tree block is referenced by a newer parent;
  - the generation-3 state has 5 of 6 blocks: its csum tree root, the mkfs leaf at 1130496 without
    the WRITTEN flag, is present but invalid. The N = 15 measurement read it through the current
    chunk map only and classified it `unmapped`. Since the review fixes it is read through the
    state's own chunk items and checked against invalid scanned copies, and it is `corrupt`
    (§6.6). That state and generations 6–16 lie in the pre-balance and mkfs chunks, which the
    current chunk map no longer covers. Their inferred historical chunk trees (generations 3, 6,
    17, 23, 24, 29, 30) place every found block where it was scanned.
- **sync.** 2 candidate root-tree blocks (states):
  - generation 38 (current and backup 38), complete (10 of 10);
  - generation 3, the same mkfs-era state as above (5 of 6);
  - **lost:** the root, extent, chunk and dev tree roots of backups 35, 36 and 37, all reading as
    zeros (2 copies each). That is 12 of the 26 slot references but 8 distinct blocks: root ×3,
    extent ×3, chunk 131104768 and dev 64847872. The fs root (generation 19) and csum root
    (generation 35) of those backups are shared with the live state and survive. Hence **26 slot
    references (14 distinct blocks); 14 of 26 references, but only 6 of 14 distinct blocks,
    survive under sync**;
  - the 16 surviving valid orphans are all outside the current chunk map, at 1–1.2 MiB,
    5.0–5.4 MiB, 22020096 and 30408704, with generations 2–5. mkfs finishes at generation 6
    (research.md §10.4), so all of them are mkfs residue. **No block freed by the kernel during the
    scenario survives.**

### 6.4 What the numbers show

- Counted under the probe's rules, btrfska's full-sweep candidates give exactly the probe's counts
  on 48 images, once each tool's skip ranges and counting rules are made explicit. That is
  coverage agreement (§2): btrfska reads the probe's offsets, matches the same fsid bytes and reads
  the same generation field. The M2 DoD agreement bullet is met without any tolerance, in that
  sense.
- On this scenario, **`discard=sync` removed all history the kernel itself freed**. Without trims,
  old-root discovery resolves 31 candidate root-tree blocks (states) beyond the 4 backup-root
  states a backup-root-bounded tool can reach, 30 of them with every referenced block found, and
  every backup state is complete. Why those 31 survive is scenario-specific (§6.5). With sync
  discard, the backup states older than the last commit lose their root-tree block, and only
  mkfs-era residue and the live state remain.
- The kernel code is consistent with this. Under `DISCARD_SYNC`, `btrfs_finish_extent_commit`
  (v7.0 extent-tree.c:2997-3005) discards every extent range unpinned at the end of each
  transaction commit, which includes tree blocks freed by copy-on-write in that transaction. It also
  discards every block group deleted in the transaction (l.3058-3063), such as the chunks the
  balance emptied (block-group.c:1755-1767 freezes them for that trim). A superseded tree block
  therefore survives only until the commit that frees it. This mechanism is read from the source,
  not measured separately.
- Async discard with an unmount about a second after the last write left every block in place in
  all 15 runs (no measurable difference from no discard on any class or discovery metric). That is
  consistent with the async queue delaying its work (`btrfs_discard_schedule_work`,
  extent-tree.c:3048-3051; research.md §10.3).

### 6.5 What the numbers do not show

- **Not a general survival rate for superseded states.** The 31 states beyond the backups survive
  for two reasons specific to this scenario:
  - generations 3–16 lie in the mkfs and pre-balance chunks that the scenario's last operation, a
    full balance, relocated and deleted. No block of those states is placed by the current chunk
    map (`maps_current` 0), their device ranges went back to unallocated space, and nothing
    reallocated them before unmount;
  - generations 17–34 lie in the metadata block group the balance created (logical 63963136,
    64 MiB). Every root-tree block of generations 17–38 lies within its first 1.41 MiB. The
    allocator continues from its last allocation (extent-tree.c:4235-4241 records it as the
    cluster's `window_start`, l.4451-4466 takes it as the hint, l.4620-4622 starts the search
    there), so in this young, mostly empty block group it never came back to the blocks those
    generations freed. This mechanism is read from the source, not measured.

  Survival therefore depends on the balance and on the filesystem's short life (38 transactions).
  It should not be generalised: an aged filesystem, one without a balance, or one whose metadata
  block group wraps around would keep fewer states.
- **Not file-level recoverability.** Completeness counts tree blocks of the root tree and of the
  ROOT_ITEM-named trees, resolved among scanned blocks; the chunk tree, the log tree and
  ROOT_ITEMs naming tree 1 are excluded. It does not read file extents, check data checksums or
  verify content (M4, M6). On these small images every root tree is a single leaf and most trees
  are one block deep, so completeness is easy to reach; deeper trees will expose more missing
  blocks. Nothing is known below a missing block, so completeness overstates survival.
- **Not SSD behaviour.** TRIM here punches holes in a sparse raw file on ext4 through virtio-blk,
  so trimmed blocks read as zeros. A real SSD may return zeros, old data or indeterminate data for a
  trimmed LBA, and chip-off acquisition may still see flash contents. "Zeroed" is a statement about
  the image a host acquires through such a block layer.
- **Not async discard in general.** Only a quick unmount was measured; an idle filesystem trims
  asynchronously and would lose history too (plan.md M7 "async idle" row).
- **Not other scenarios, sizes or kernels.** One 512 MiB scenario with 38 transactions and a full
  balance, stock 7.0 guest, host mkfs 6.6.3. The survival of 31 states without trims reflects a
  quiescent image; later writes would reuse many of those blocks (`reused` in walk failures, as on
  `m2_logtree`).
- **Not the "no discard" mount option.** The none row's guest mounts with `discard=async`; the host
  drops the TRIMs (EXP-000 §3).
- **Not the cause of the run-6 jitter**, nor whether rarer outcomes exist beyond 15 runs.

## 7. Threats to validity

- **Internal.**
  - The compatibility count reuses btrfska's scan plan and FSID prefilter. A bug shared by that
    offset iteration and prefilter would therefore affect both sides of the btrfska comparison
    alike, although the probe itself is separate standard-library Python. The agreement verifies
    coverage (§2), not btrfska's validation or classification.
  - Discovery results are identical within each mode, so run-to-run guest jitter did not reach
    them here.
- **External.** See §6.5: survival that depends on the final balance and the short life of the
  filesystem, virtio sparse-file TRIM semantics, one small scenario, stock kernel, no aging, quick
  unmount.
- **Construct.**
  - A "state" is a candidate root-tree block (§2): an owner-1 block no parent of its generation or
    a newer one points to. It can be an older leaf of a multi-leaf root tree whose parent is gone,
    covering that leaf's ROOT_ITEMs only, or a forged block; `level_consistent` flags one kind of
    forgery. On these images every root-tree block is a level-0 root, so each state is a whole
    root tree.
  - "Complete" means every block of the root tree and of every ROOT_ITEM-named tree was found. It
    excludes the chunk tree, the log tree and ROOT_ITEMs naming tree 1, and is relative to what
    the found blocks reference.
  - "26 roots" are slot references; they name 14 distinct blocks (§6.3).

## 8. Status

- **Agreement: supports, as coverage agreement.** 48 of 48 images give equal counts (M2 DoD met,
  §2 for what that verifies).
- **Discard and history: observation**, deterministic per mode across 15 regenerations. Sync
  discard left 2 of 35 candidate root-tree blocks (the live one and mkfs residue), 0
  backup-reachable blocks, and 14 of 26 slot references, which is 6 of 14 distinct blocks. None and
  async left all 35 states, 31 of them beyond the backup roots, for reasons specific to the
  scenario's balance and short life (§6.5).
- **Follow-ups:** the async-idle and `nodiscard` rows (M7); deeper trees, aged filesystems and
  file-level recovery per state (M4/M5); a real-SSD acquisition; historical chunk maps for the
  pre-balance states (M5).
