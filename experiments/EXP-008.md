# EXP-008 — Joining orphan blocks: what the orphan graph adds, and whether any join is wrong

- **Date:** 2026-09-21 (registration)
- **Milestone:** M5c-1 (plan.md "M5c: the orphan graph"); claim C1 (orphan sources), and the
  third source of C3's timelines
- **Script:** [`experiments/exp008.py`](exp008.py); environment: [`experiments/env.sh`](env.sh)
- **Raw outputs:** `images/scratch/exp/EXP-008/results.jsonl` (gitignored), one line per build
- **Registration:** §1 and §2 were committed before any join was implemented. **Not blind:** a
  survey of one `m4_deep` build on the same day (plan.md M5c) had shown that 303 of 345 orphan
  leaves are named by a scanned key pointer, that 110 file-tree nodes are named by nothing, and
  that `recover --orphans` leaves 134 files `partial` with `continues_elsewhere` and 324
  `complete` files without a path. No join had been computed.

## 1. Hypothesis

Most of what `recover --orphans` reads leaf by leaf belongs to tree versions that were written
within a transaction and replaced before its commit, and can be joined on the evidence of key
pointers; what remains alone can sometimes be joined to a sibling or given a path; and the rules
of plan.md M5c refuse every join that would be wrong.

- **H1 (pointer joins dominate).** At least 80 % of the orphan leaves of a build lie under a
  fragment (a file-tree block that no pointer, ROOT_ITEM or superblock slot names).
- **H2 (cut files).** Of the files `--orphans` leaves `partial` with `continues_elsewhere`, at
  least half come out `complete` with `--graph`, through a fragment or a `sibling` join.
- **H3 (paths).** Of the `complete` orphan files without a path under `--orphans`, at least half
  have one with `--graph`.
- **H4 (no wrong join).** Registered as zero in every build, for each of three checks:
  (a) a `complete` `orphan_graph` file with the name of a logged file (victim, flash, open-
  unlinked) and another hash than the logged one, size-0 versions aside (EXP-007 §6.2);
  (b) a `complete` `orphan_graph` padding file `pad/pN` whose content is none of those the
  scenario can have written to `pN` (`seq 1 300`, or `seq r (300 + r)` for a round `r` with
  `N ≡ r mod 7 + 1 (mod 7)`);
  (c) an `orphan_graph` file that an anchored root also holds with the same tree, inode,
  creation generation and `transid`, but with another extent signature.
- **H5 (refusals happen and are visible).** Some joins are refused, and every refusal is in the
  artifact's problems with its reason. Registered: at least one refusal per build; no prediction
  of how many.

**What would refute it.** Any count above zero in H4: then a rule lets through a file that never
existed, and `orphan_graph` cannot be offered as evidence until the rule is fixed. H1 to H3
failing would mean the graph adds little on this kind of history, which would be reported as
such.

**What it means for the paper.** C1's orphan sources become usable at file level (names, whole
files), with the provenance of every join; the paper can say how much of an image's
unreferenced metadata is explained by never-committed tree versions.

## 2. Method

**Scenario.** `corpus/vm/scenarios/deep.guest.sh`, the recipe of `m4_deep` (EXP-006 §2): 48
subvolumes, 400 inline padding files of which a seventh is rewritten per round, 24 victims, 8
flash files, one file unlinked while open, power-off without unmount. Every content is
deterministic, so every recovered padding file can be checked against the scenario.

**Builds.** Five fresh builds, each measured and deleted, and the corpus image `m4_deep` as a
sixth line. `m3_wide` (no log tree, deletions between commits) is measured once for H1 to H3.

**Measurement per build.** `catalog build --full-sweep`; `recover --root all --tree all
--orphans` and, into another directory, `recover --root all --tree all --graph`. Orphan leaves
under a fragment are counted from the second run's provenance. Files are matched between the
runs by (tree, inode, creation generation, `transid`, leaf of the INODE_ITEM). H4 is evaluated on
the second run as defined above. The image's hash is taken before and after.

**Reported.** Median and range over the five builds, the host, and any bound that bit.

## 2a. Amendment before the builds (2026-09-21)

A trial of the finished script on the two corpus images, before any of the five builds, showed
that two of the three checks of H4 do not measure what they were meant to. It is recorded here
with its numbers, because the registered checks fail as worded.

- **What the trial showed** (`m4_deep`, one build): H4(a) 0 of 22; **H4(b) 225 of 37 743** padding
  files with a content the scenario cannot have written; **H4(c) 316 of 44 326** files with
  another extent signature than the anchored file of the same inode, creation generation and
  `transid` (`m3_wide`: 192 of 19 334).
- **None of them is a join.** Every one of the 316 is a file whose items lie in a single leaf of
  a fragment. 247 hold an extent that is *newer than their own INODE_ITEM*: the leaf was written
  to disk after the data was rewritten and before the inode item was updated (btrfs updates it
  through delayed items, at the commit at the latest), so the new data is cut to the old size.
  That is what H4(b) found, too: `pad/p298` reads `seq 24 324` cut at the 1 121 bytes of the
  version before. The other 69 are size-0 versions (truncated, not yet rewritten) within the same
  transaction as the anchored version. So (tree, inode, creation generation, `transid`) does not
  identify a version of a file outside committed trees, and H4(c) was built on that assumption.
  M4's `recover --orphans` has read these leaves in the same way since M4d and called them
  `complete`.
- **What changed in the tool, because of it.** A file with an extent newer than its INODE_ITEM is
  no longer `complete`: it is `partial`, reason `inode_item_older_than_extent`, for every source.
  On seven images (every state, deduplication off) no anchored root holds such a file, which is
  what the commit guarantees; only uncommitted leaves do.
- **The checks as amended, registered now, before the builds.** H4(a) is unchanged. **H4(b2):**
  as (b), which now sees such files as `partial`, so it counts only files called `complete`:
  registered 0. **H4(c2):** as (c), but only for `complete` files whose items come from two
  leaves or more, the only files whose *content* a join assembled: registered 0. The registered
  (b) and (c) are still computed and reported; (c) is expected to stay far above zero, for the
  reason above, and that is reported as the refutation of the check, not of the joins.
- H1 to H3 and H5 are unchanged. The trial's values for them (`m4_deep`: 303 of 345 leaves under
  a fragment; 133 of 134 cut files complete; 2 757 of 3 137 pathless files with a path) were seen
  before the builds, so the builds add the spread, not the surprise.
